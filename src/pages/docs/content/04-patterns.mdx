All layout options except the radial pattern (see [07 Radial layout](#/docs/radial)) are all built from the same three constants, computed once from the pitch:

1. **row spacing** ($d_x$): the spacing between neighbouring tubes *within the same row*.
2. **row-to-row spacing** ($d_y$): the vertical spacing between one row and the next.
3. **row stagger** ($C$): the horizontal offset applied to every other row. This is what staggers the pattern instead of stacking it in a plain grid (see [06 Filling the circle](#/docs/filling)).

A tube’s nearest neighbour usually sits in the *adjacent* row rather than its own (see [02 Pitch & pitch ratio](#/docs/pitch)), so the pitch would be the diagonal distance to that neighbour, not from $d_x$ or $d_y$ directly:

:::Formula{id="pattern-pitch" label="Pitch, derived from dx, dy, C"}
$$
\begin{align}
P_t = \sqrt{C^2 + d_y^2}
\end{align}
$$
:::

The layout’s name (30°, 45°, 60°, or 90°) is the angle ($\theta$) between that diagonal pitch line and the vertical, row-to-row direction:

:::Formula{id="layout-angle" label="Layout angle"}
$$
\begin{align}
\theta = \arctan\!\left( \frac{C}{d_y} \right)
\end{align}
$$
:::

The table below gives each layout’s $d_x$, $d_y$, and $C$ in terms of the pitch, followed by a diagram of the same numbers. Each diagram draws the actual nearest-neighbour tubes: an apex tube plus two base tubes for the 30°/60° triangular patterns, or four tubes forming a square or diamond for the 45°/90° square patterns. The highlighted lines are true pitch-length sides, and the arc marks the angle.

:::Table{id="pattern-constants" caption="Layout constants (dx, dy, C) by pattern angle."}
| Layout | $d_x$ | $d_y$ | $C$ | Row spacing vs. pitch |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | --- |
| 30° | $P_t$ | $\dfrac{P_t}{\sin 60°}$ | $\dfrac{P_t}{2}$ | equal ($d_x = P_t$) |
| 60° | $\dfrac{2P_t}{\sin 60°}$ | $\dfrac{P_t}{2}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $d_x$ greater than $P_t$ |
| 90° | $P_t$ | $P_t$ | $0$ (square) | equal ($d_x = P_t$) |
| 45° | $\dfrac{P_t}{\cos 45°}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $d_x$ greater than $P_t$ |
:::

30° and 60° are the two equilateral-triangle (“triangular pitch”) patterns, rotated relative to each other. Each tube sits exactly one pitch from the nearest vertically or horizontally aligned tube.

45° and 90° are the same square (“diamond”) pattern in two orientations. Every side of that square is one pitch long, so its corner angle is always 90°, regardless of rotation.
