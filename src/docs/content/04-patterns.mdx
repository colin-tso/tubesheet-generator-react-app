Every layout option except the radial pattern (see [07 Radial layout](#/docs/radial-layout)) is built from the same three constants unique to each layout, computed once from the pitch:

1. **row spacing** ($d_x$): the spacing between neighbouring tubes *within the same row*.
2. **row-to-row spacing** ($d_y$): the vertical spacing between one row and the next.
3. **row stagger** ($C$): the horizontal offset applied to every other row. Offsetting alternate rows staggers the tubes rather than aligning them into a plain grid (see [06 Filling the circle](#/docs/filling)).

A tube’s nearest neighbour usually sits in the *adjacent* row rather than its own (see [02 Pitch and pitch ratio](#/docs/pitch)), so the pitch is the diagonal distance to that neighbour rather than $d_x$ or $d_y$:

:::Formula{id="pattern-pitch" label="Pitch, derived from dx, dy, C"}
$$
\begin{align}
P_t = \sqrt{C^2 + d_y^2}
\end{align}
$$
:::

The layout’s name (30°, 45°, 60°, or 90°) is the angle between the nearest-neighbour pitch line and the vertical, row-to-row direction. For the 30°, 60°, and 45° layouts the pitch line runs diagonally, and this angle is given by:

:::Formula{id="layout-angle" label="Layout angle"}
$$
\begin{align}
\theta = \arctan\!\left( \frac{C}{d_y} \right)
\end{align}
$$
:::

<Note>These four layouts are the standard tube-sheet patterns set out in [TEMA](https://www.tema.org) Standards (see [10 Glossary](#/docs/glossary)): 30° and 60° are the two triangular-pitch patterns, and 45° and 90° are the two square-pitch patterns. Only the radial layout (see [07 Radial layout](#/docs/radial-layout)) falls outside these standards.</Note>

For the 90° square layout the neighbouring tubes line up horizontally, at right angles to the vertical, so the layout is named for that 90° angle rather than for a diagonal pitch line.

The table below gives each layout’s $d_x$, $d_y$, and $C$ in terms of the pitch, and the diagrams below show the same values. Each diagram draws the actual nearest-neighbour tubes: an apex tube plus two base tubes for the 30°/60° triangular patterns, or four tubes forming a square or diamond for the 45°/90° square patterns. The highlighted lines are true pitch-length sides, and the arc marks the angle.

:::Table{id="pattern-constants" caption="Layout constants (dx, dy, C) by pattern angle."}
| Layout | $d_x$ | $d_y$ | $C$ | Row spacing vs. pitch |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | --- |
| 30° | $P_t$ | $P_t\cdot\sin 60°$ | $\dfrac{P_t}{2}$ | equal<wbr /><span className="docs-table-nowrap"> ($d_x = P_t$)</span> |
| 60° | $2P_t\cdot\sin 60°$ | $\dfrac{P_t}{2}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $d_x$ greater than $P_t$ |
| 90° | $P_t$ | $P_t$ | $0$<wbr /><span className="docs-table-nowrap"> (square)</span> | equal<wbr /><span className="docs-table-nowrap"> ($d_x = P_t$)</span> |
| 45° | $\dfrac{P_t}{\cos 45°}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $\dfrac{d_x}{2}$ | $d_x$ greater than $P_t$ |
:::

30° and 60° are the two equilateral-triangle (“triangular pitch”) patterns, rotated relative to each other. In the 30° pattern, horizontally aligned tubes are exactly one pitch apart; in the 60° pattern, vertically aligned tubes are exactly one pitch apart.

45° and 90° are the same square pattern in two orientations. In the 90° layout the nearest-neighbour lines run horizontally and vertically; in the 45° layout the pattern is rotated so those lines sit at 45° to the horizontal and vertical.
