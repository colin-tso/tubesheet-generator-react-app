Radial is a different pattern entirely. Instead of rows and columns, it places tubes on **concentric rings** centred in the tube sheet. On each ring, tubes sit evenly spaced so that neighbouring tubes on the same ring are exactly one pitch apart, and the rings are spaced one pitch apart from one another, so every tube keeps at least one pitch of clearance from the rings on either side.

A ring of radius $r$ can hold at most $n$ tubes, where the chord between neighbouring tubes, $2 r \sin\!\left( \frac{\pi}{n} \right)$, is at least one pitch. Rounding down gives the **ring tube capacity**:

:::Formula{id="radial-ring-tube-count" label="Ring tube capacity"}
$$
\begin{align}
n_\text{ring} = \left\lfloor \pi \cdot {\left[\arcsin\!\left(\frac{P_t}{2r}\right)\right]^{-1}} \right\rfloor
\end{align}
$$
:::

Conversely, the radius needed to place *exactly* $n$ tubes on a ring, so the chord between neighbouring tubes is exactly one pitch, is:

:::Formula{id="radial-ring-radius" label="Ring radius for n tubes"}
$$
\begin{align}
r_n = \frac{P_t}{2 \sin\!\left( \dfrac{\pi}{n} \right)}
\end{align}
$$
:::

and the **angle** between neighbouring tubes on that ring:

:::Formula{id="radial-ring-angle" label="Angle between tubes"}
$$
\begin{align}
\theta_\text{radial} = \frac{2\pi}{n}
\end{align}
$$
:::

Instead of stepping through rows and columns until they fall outside the boundary (see [06 Filling the circle](#/docs/filling)), the generator fills whole rings at once. It builds one candidate layout per **seed**, then keeps whichever candidate holds the most tubes — the same rule the grid layouts use when selecting an offset (see [05 Offsets](#/docs/offset)).

A seed is the innermost pattern: either a **central tube**, or a ring of exactly **2, 3, 4, or 5 tubes** placed at the smallest radius that keeps its tubes exactly one pitch apart (see <EqRef id="radial-ring-radius" />).

Starting from the seed, the generator places full rings **one pitch further out** at each step ($r_\text{seed} + P_t,\ r_\text{seed} + 2P_t, \dots$), each holding its ring tube capacity. Every ring shares the same starting angle, so the tubes aligned radially across adjacent rings sit exactly one pitch apart and any other pair of tubes on adjacent rings is further apart. Therefore, the whole layout has a minimum clearance of one pitch between any two tubes.

The seeds span the range full rings cannot reach on their own: the two-tube ring at $P_t/2$ is the smallest ring that can exist, and the 3-, 4-, and 5-tube rings fill the gap between $P_t/2$ and $P_t$.

A minimum tube count that lands on an achievable count based on the seeds resolves to exactly that many tubes; one that falls in a gap resolves to the next achievable count, so a minimum of 6 still gives the 7-tube layout, because the six-tube ring fits in exactly the same shell as the seven-tube one.

As with the grid layouts, the smallest shell ID that meets the target is found numerically (see [08 Solving for shell ID](#/docs/solving)).
