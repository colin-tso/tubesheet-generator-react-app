As an example, consider a tube OD of 19.05 mm, a pitch ratio of 1.25, an OTL clearance of 12.7 mm, and a 30° layout.

Using <EqRef id="pitch" /> and <TableRef id="pattern-constants" />:

:::Formula{id="example-pitch" label="Pitch"}
$$
\begin{align*}
P = 19.05 \times 1.25 = 23.8125\ \text{mm}
\end{align*}
$$
:::

:::Formula{id="example-dxdyc" label="dx, dy, C (30°)"}
$$
\begin{align*}
d_x &= 23.8125\ \text{mm} \\
d_y &= 20.6230\ \text{mm} \\
C &= 11.9063\ \text{mm}
\end{align*}
$$
:::

### Number of tubes for a 500 mm shell

Using the maximum OTL (<EqRef id="otl-max" />) and the [06 Filling the circle](#/docs/filling) procedure described above, the generator produces:

:::Formula{id="example-forward-result" label="Result"}
$$
\begin{align*}
\text{OTL}_\text{max} &= 500 - 12.7 = 487.3\ \text{mm}\\
n_\text{act} &= 349 \\
\text{OTL}_\text{act} &= 478.33\ \text{mm}
\end{align*}
$$
:::

The actual OTL of 478.33 mm is below the 487.3 mm ceiling, because the discrete triangular pattern cannot fill every remaining fraction of the circle.

### Minimum shell ID for a target of 350 tubes

Solving for the smallest shell ID ($D_\text{shell, min}$, <EqRef id="solving-initial-estimate" />) that holds at least 350 tubes gives:

:::Formula{id="example-inverse-result" label="Result"}
$$
\begin{align*}
D_\text{shell, min} &= 500.20\ \text{mm} \\
n_\text{act} &= 352
\end{align*}
$$
:::

The result overshoots the 350-tube target slightly, yielding 352, since the next smaller diameter would have dropped below 350. So 500.20 mm is the tightest shell ID that still meets the target.
