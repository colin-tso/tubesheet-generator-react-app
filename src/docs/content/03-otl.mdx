**OTL** (Outer Tube Limit) is the diameter of the smallest circle that encloses every tube in the field, wall-to-wall. It bounds how far the pattern can spread:

- A maximum allowable OTL **constraint** ($\text{OTL}_\text{max}$), set by the shell ID and the OTL clearance you specify, i.e. the maximum diameter the pattern is allowed to reach.
- An actual OTL **result** ($\text{OTL}_\text{act}$), measured from the tube field the generator actually produced. This comes in smaller than the constraint whenever the discrete rows and columns cannot fill the circle exactly.

:::Formula{id="otl-max" label="Max allowable OTL"}
$$
\begin{align}
\text{OTL}_\text{max} = D_\text{shell} - \text{OTL}_\text{clear}
\end{align}
$$
:::

:::Formula{id="otl-actual" label="Actual OTL (from the generated field)"}
$$
\begin{align}
\text{OTL}_\text{act} = 2 \cdot \max\!\left( \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \right) + D_\text{tube}
\end{align}
$$
:::
Where $x$ and $y$ are the positions of all tube centres relative to the middle of the tube sheet.

The formula above finds tube centre farthest from the middle of the tube sheet and adds one tube radius on each side of that centre-to-centre distance, resulting in the actual OTL. The generator only accepts a tube centre if its distance from the origin keeps it within $( \text{OTL}_\text{max} - D_\text{tube} ) \div 2$, i.e. within the max allowable OTL once the tube’s own radius is accounted for.
