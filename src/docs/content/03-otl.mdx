**OTL** (Outer Tube Limit) is the diameter of the smallest circle that encloses every tube in the field, wall-to-wall. It bounds how far the pattern can spread:

- A maximum allowable OTL **constraint** ($\text{OTL}_\text{max}$), set by the shell ID and the OTL clearance entered in the calculator: the maximum diameter the pattern may reach. The clearance is diametrical, so each side of the shell leaves $\text{OTL}_\text{clear} / 2$ between the outermost tube wall and the shell ID.
- An actual OTL **result** ($\text{OTL}_\text{act}$), measured from the tube field the generator actually produced. The actual OTL is below the constraint whenever the discrete pattern cannot fill the circle exactly.

:::Formula{id="otl-max" label="Max allowable OTL"}
$$
\begin{align}
\text{OTL}_\text{max} = D_\text{shell} - \text{OTL}_\text{clear}
\end{align}
$$
:::

:::Formula{id="otl-actual" label="Actual OTL (from the generated field)"}
$$
\begin{align}
\text{OTL}_\text{act} = 2 \cdot \max\!\left( \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \right) + D_\text{tube}
\end{align}
$$
:::
where $x$ and $y$ are the positions of all tube centres relative to the centre of the tube sheet.

This formula finds the tube centre farthest from the centre of the tube sheet and adds one tube radius on each side of that centre-to-centre distance, resulting in the actual OTL. The generator accepts a tube centre only if its distance from the centre is at most $( \text{OTL}_\text{max} - D_\text{tube} ) \div 2$, so the whole tube, including its radius, stays within the maximum allowable OTL.
