The **pitch** ($P_t$) is the centre-to-centre distance between neighbouring tubes.

In most engineering scenarios a **pitch ratio** ($P_\text{ratio}$) is used instead, which is pitch expressed as a multiple of the tube OD ($D_\text{tube}$). Expressing pitch as a ratio of the tube OD lets the same relative spacing scale with tube size.

:::Formula{id="pitch" label="Pitch"}
$$
\begin{align}
P_t = D_\text{tube} \times P_\text{ratio}
\end{align}
$$
:::

The gap between adjacent tube walls, not centres, is the **tube clearance** or **pitch clearance** ($P_\text{clear}$). It is similar to pitch but measured wall-to-wall instead of centre-to-centre:

:::Formula{id="pitch-clearance" label="Tube clearance from pitch ratio"}
$$
\begin{align}
P_\text{clear} &= P_t - D_\text{tube} \\
               &= (P_\text{ratio} - 1) \times D_\text{tube}
\end{align}
$$
:::

:::Formula{id="pitch-ratio-from-clearance" label="Pitch ratio from tube clearance"}
$$
\begin{align}
P_\text{ratio} = 1 + \cfrac{P_\text{clear}} {D_\text{tube}}
\end{align}
$$
:::

As the equations show, pitch, pitch ratio and tube clearance are related; each can be calculated from either of the others.

A pitch ratio of 1 corresponds to tubes touching wall-to-wall (zero clearance). Values below 1 are not physically possible and are rejected.
